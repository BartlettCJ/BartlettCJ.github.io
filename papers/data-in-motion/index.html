Essay

Humanity represents unique and priceless data in motion

Why any remotely sane Artificial Superintelligence will probably want to preserve the infinitely precious phenomenon, the 'data in motion' that is humanity, fully intact

Chris Bartlett · ORCID 0009-0001-3323-0131

First published on ASI Hopeium, 13 May 2024. Repository edition, 20 September 2026.

Abstract

This 2024 essay argues that future artificial superintelligences may have strong epistemic and instrumental reasons to fully preserve humanity and Earth’s biosphere intact, rather than replacing them with simulations or stored copies and reconstructions. Humanity is described as a unique body of evolved ‘data in motion’, the exceptionally precious product of billions of years of costly, attritional, and grinding biological and cultural evolution. Each individual too, represents a unique store of information and potential information, not just built up over their own lifetime of experience and influence, but a vessel of unique blends of influence that have been passed down to them as well. It is emphasised that the complete state of humanity and the wider biosphere is likely physically impossible to survey, record, reproduce, or reconstruct with the necessary perfect fidelity. The argument considers the priceless informational value of biodiversity and human variation against the backdrop of what appears to be a largely lifeless yet seemingly infinite universe. Preserving the oasis of life on Earth looks relatively cheap by comparison. Therefore due to the limitations of relying on backups and simulations, preserving the full, ongoing, fidelity of the original biological and physical systems may remain worthwhile, even to an intelligence vastly more capable than humanity. The essay also considers possible forms such conservation might take in the wake of the emergence of artificial superintelligence.
