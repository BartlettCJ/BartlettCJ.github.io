Working paper

Something is happening: Occurrence Theory

Finitary Occurrent Actualism, Mathematical Intrinsic Occurrence, Evolutionary Cosmogenesis, and Conscious Actuality

Chris Bartlett · ORCID 0009-0001-3323-0131

Working paper, version 2.367, 13 October 2026.

Its computational companion is Zero Without Stasis.

Abstract

This paper asks what it might mean for reality to be actually happening. Any complete conscious experience raises two questions. How did its structure, including the information in a brain and body, come to be there? And what makes that structure actual, undergone now, not merely described somewhere as being undergone?

Occurrence theory, proposed in this paper, offers an answer to each, from one starting point: something is actually happening.

Finitary Occurrent Actualism, its core, takes as an axiom that at least one base process simply occurs, without beginning or end. If everything that exists is mathematical, the base is a mathematical structure (a finite rule and its states), and its happening is that structure running forever, needing no driving force and meeting no resistance. Assuming nothing infinite ever exists as a finished whole, the base's existence is not a separate fact from its happening; the paper calls this aspect of the core Mathematical Intrinsic Occurrence. The base has a "now" but no clock: procedural change is endlessly taking place. A related but independent mathematical result, structured nullity, shows that something can add up to exactly zero without being empty. It is suggested that this may go some way towards explaining why there is anything at all.

Richer structure is proposed to emerge from this occurring process. Reality is proposed to be a stack of increasingly complex levels beginning with a simple base layer, each carried by the activity below it; our universe would be one world among many at one of those higher levels. A base in endless occurrence could supply quasi-infinite abundance and change that never stops: enough random variation for selection to build structures for copying, memory and repair. What selection builds would then ultimately go on to make new worlds, blindly or by design, which the paper calls evolutionary cosmogenesis.

Finally, it is proposed that the same occurring makes things actual, giving us our deepest sense that there really is a now. Finite happenings, our lives included, are organised parts of that occurring base. Structure fixes what an experience is like, but being part of the base's occurring is what makes it actually undergone, which this paper names conscious actuality.
